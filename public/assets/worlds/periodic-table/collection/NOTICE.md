# Periodic table photo collection

The active collection contains 100 distinct photographic references for 101 element positions; tennessine explicitly shows the berkelium target material used in its synthesis. Seventeen other positions use separately labelled original synthesis drawings in `../synthesis/`, not photographs. The former generic `cyclotron-60in.webp` is retained with its historical source record but is no longer used by any element tile.

Most source photographs come from Chemical Elements — A Virtual Museum (https://images-of-elements.com/), whose selected pages state CC BY 3.0 unless a page explicitly marks the image Public Domain. The americium metal chunks photograph is credited to Los Alamos National Laboratory via Wikimedia Commons and marked Public Domain under the U.S. DOE work notice. The actinium, curium, thorium and francium photographs are by James St. John under CC BY 2.0. The promethium photograph is credited to Oak Ridge National Laboratory and marked Public Domain. Each claim and credit applies only to the specifically recorded file, not automatically to all images published by that institution.

The six replacements added on 2026-09-10 are:

- **Po:** NASA / Glenn Research Center / Bill Bowles, 1957 cloud-chamber experiment with a polonium alpha source, Public Domain. Condensation tracks are observed, not bulk polonium metal.
- **At:** NuclearAlex, cropped by Mithoron, CC BY 4.0; approximately 40 ng of At-211 at Forschungszentrum Jülich, photographed in January 2026. The blue glow is radiation-induced ionisation in a roughly two-minute exposure (EXIF: 106 seconds), not the element's ordinary colour. The source record links the uncropped photograph too. No claim of being the first-ever photo is made.
- **Rn:** Curt Storlazzi / USGS, Public Domain; a radon-detecting buoy tracing submarine groundwater discharge at Ofu, American Samoa. This is a specifically identified radon measurement, not a visible sample of the gas.
- **Fr:** James St. John, CC BY 2.0; a uranium mineral identified by the photographer as containing minute quantities of francium. The orange solid is mineral, not francium metal. The photo does not measure francium concentration.
- **Th:** James St. John, CC BY 2.0; a dark metal sample in a sealed glass tube in a thorium display, with no reported purity or dimensions.
- **Pa:** U.S. Department of Energy, Public Domain; a circa-1969 Pa-233 experimental sample photographed using light from its own radioactive emission. The black-and-white image does not establish ordinary visible-light colour.

Files are local resized WebP derivatives; no pixels in this directory were generated, reconstructed, recoloured or composited by AI. Earlier originals were reduced to a maximum 720px edge at q82; the six additions use a maximum 1280px edge at q86. `sources.json` records source page, original download URL, credit, licence, retrieval date, hash and conversion. Earlier low-resolution source thumbnails remain explicitly marked. Source photos for the 82 AI display adaptations remain unchanged.
