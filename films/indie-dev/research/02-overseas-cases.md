# 02 - Overseas indie developers: public revenue case studies

Research date: 2026-10-21. All figures are as shown on the pages named, on or before that date. Nothing here is invented; where a number could not be found it says "not public" or "not located".

Label key (as requested):
- [SELF-REPORTED] the person's own public claim (own blog, own post, own open page)
- [VERIFIED] payment-provider-verified (TrustMRR reads Stripe/RevenueCat read-only; shown with "last updated" date) or a third-party-confirmed deal (e.g. TechCrunch/Wix)
- [MEDIA] reported by a third party (newsletter, case-study site, podcast summary, estimate site); treat as unconfirmed
- [DERIVED] my arithmetic from numbers above

Reading tips for the film team:
- TrustMRR is owned by Marc Lou. It is the best public verification source, but it shows MRR (subscriptions) and 30-day revenue, so one-time-sale businesses look smaller than they are. Some TrustMRR pages carry a "Stripe key expired" note (flagged where seen).
- Several "profile" sites are low reliability: Starter Story business pages are AI-compiled from public sources and say so; Latka numbers are estimates; dev.to "promptway" posts, yespress.io, stork.ai, stealwhatworks.com, buildmvpfast.com are secondary aggregators. They are used only where nothing better was reachable, and are labelled [MEDIA].
- I could not open X/Twitter directly. Follower counts come from TrustMRR founder displays (Oct 21 2026) or from secondary sources, and are labelled.
- The WebSearch budget for this session ran out mid-task. Candidates I did not get to are listed in section 9 so they can be picked up in a follow-up.

## 0. How the roster was chosen (survivorship bias warning)

Roster = 23 people. It was assembled from three pools:
1. Names the brief asked for (Pieter Levels, Marc Lou, Tony Dinh, Danny Postma, Jon Yongfook, Damon Chen, Arvid Kahl, Maor Shlomo). Sabba Keynejad was checked and excluded (see section 8).
2. Founders on the TrustMRR top-50 leaderboard (fetched Oct 21 2026) whose identity and numbers were findable: Chatbase, GojiberryAI, Postiz, 1Lookup, Rezi, Stan, Codedex, DataExpert, Post Bridge, SuperX.
3. People documented as "launched many, earned little" or "earn mainly from courses/templates/communities": Daniel Vassallo, Justin Welsh, Adam Wathan (Tailwind), Josh Pigford (Maybe), Alexander Belogubov (26 projects, $115K), plus the failure histories inside Levels, Lou, Hallam, Chen, Dinh, Yongfook.

Why this is biased:
- Everyone in the roster is famous enough to be written about OR publishes revenue. People who tried and quit silently are invisible. A person who earns $0 does not put up a dashboard.
- TrustMRR is self-selected (founders opt in) and mostly founded after Oct 2025, so earlier failures are missing from it.
- Famous build-in-public names are not the biggest earners. The top of TrustMRR is mostly low-profile B2B founders (see section 7).
- The 15 roster members with a comparable monthly revenue figure have a median of about $145K/month (all) or about $235K/month (verified/self-reported only). The platform-wide median 30-day revenue among TrustMRR products that earned anything is $169 (section 6). So the roster is roughly 700-1,400x the typical earner [DERIVED]. Do not present the roster as typical.

## 7. Observations useful for the video script

1. Audience before product is common among famous names, not universal among top earners. Marc Lou went 192 -> 40K+ followers in about 16 months before ShipFast; Tony Dinh 8K -> 50K+ in about 19 months before TypingMind; Rob Hallam spent about 2.5 years on X before SuperX and his audience doubled after launch. But Chatbase started from 16 followers, and the four low-profile TrustMRR earners above have 1.6K-15.6K followers.
2. Most famous names have a high failure count before the hit (Levels about 19+ Failed of ~116; Lou 38 vs 14 earning; Hallam 5 at $0; Chen 4; Belogubov 18 of 26 at $0).
3. The highest-earning famous solo names are the ones that sell to builders or creators (Lou, Vassallo, Welsh) or run consumer apps with a huge audience (Levels, Dinh).
4. AI has opened new fast-money paths (Base44, Chatbase, Postiz) and removed old ones (ShipFast, CodeFast, Tailwind Plus docs funnel). Pieter's fly.pieter.com is a case of fast money that did not last.
5. Revenue disclosures are cherry-picked in time: Levels posts record months, Lou posts launches; verified numbers from TrustMRR often show lower monthly totals than headlines.
