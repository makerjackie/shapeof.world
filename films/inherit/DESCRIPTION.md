## YouTube 英文简介

Does wealth really disappear in three generations? A four-minute silent data film from The Shape of the World (世界的形状), told with old photographs and a golden family tree.

Money fades fast. Of the 400 richest Americans on the 1982 Forbes list, only 34 were still on it in 2014. When a grandfather was among the wealthiest 1 percent of Americans, 93 of 100 grandchildren fell out of it.

Social status tells a different story. Thirteen rare surnames that produced many Qing-dynasty scholars were, relative to their share of the population, still 1.6 times as common as local common surnames among Tsinghua freshmen more than a century later. Leading families in Jiangnan took over three hundred years to return to the average. After China's land reform divided landlords' estates, their grandchildren earned 16 percent more than others, and about 14.5 percent were back in the top 10 percent of earners, close to about 14.1 percent in the United States.

What gets passed down is upbringing. Money lasts three generations; social status takes three hundred years to fade.
