# Nuclear-bomb · sources and scope

This is a museum-level illustration of the order of layers and events. Geometry is original and deliberately not to scale. It contains no engineering specifications. Source pages were checked on 2026-10-23.

## Mathematical and visual simplifications

- Each generation has twice as many fissions as the preceding one. This is an idealized branching model; the displayed two or three emitted neutrons do not imply that every neutron causes another fission.
- After `g` generations, the cumulative count is `2^g − 1`. Values between whole generations are a smooth interpolation. A 15 kt reference energy therefore requires approximately `1.96 × 10^24` fissions, about 81 generations and about 0.81 microseconds. The value is a teaching example, not a prediction for the displayed historical casing.
- The last `m` generations account for approximately `1 − 2^(−m)` of the total energy. The small finite-series correction is negligible at the illustrated generation count. The code computes these results; the time and energy axes remain linear.
- The chemical segment has a separate, unitless storyboard coordinate. Its screen duration is an editorial choice. No physical duration or slowdown factor is assigned to that segment.
- Chain playback gradually changes from 2.5 to 0.3 screen seconds per generation. The slowdown readout uses the instantaneous speed, including the user-selected playback rate.
- Internal layer thicknesses, spacing, displayed compression, particle paths, particle size, and heat colors are chosen for legibility. They do not reproduce measured structures, temperatures or motion. The heat field represents cumulative energy, not a temperature calculation.
- The first twelve generations are a seeded abstract illustration in a pale blue-grey field of nuclei. Its spherical front is a visual arrangement, not a spatial simulation. Generations 12–14 crossfade back to the closed cutaway; internal emission then represents cumulative energy. Changing time only samples this fixed illustration. No particle state is accumulated.
- At the endpoint the physical clock freezes. A separate two-second presentation clock fades white back to a dim, closed cutaway with a residual red glow; it can also be played backward. The numerical summary stays in the readout panel. Scrubbing directly to the physical endpoint shows its white frame. No numerical “disassembly duration” is added to the physical clock.
- The cutaway and chain-reaction geometry and animation are programmatically authored for this project; these two scenes incorporate no third-party models, images or textures. Map and globe data have separate credits below.

## Exterior reference

The [National Naval Aviation Museum display article](https://navalaviationmuseum.org/museum-marches-to-commemorate-end-of-world-war-ii/) documents a Fat Man exterior replica. The elongated painted shell and open tail frame are broad visual references only. The decorative flange, bolts and museum cradles are original display geometry; no historical engineering dimensions are reproduced. No source image is embedded in the experience.

## Gun-type and two-stage schematics

The gun-type and two-stage (Teller–Ulam) panels in "An instant" are original flat drawings for this world. They show only what general encyclopedias and museum exhibits show: the names of the major parts and the order of events. Reference points are the public explanatory diagrams in the Wikipedia articles "Gun-type fission weapon" and "Teller–Ulam design" and Britannica's entry on nuclear weapons. No artwork was copied.

The drawings carry no dimensions, masses, material quantities, component geometry, timings or other engineering parameters. Proportions exist only for legibility, and the panels say so. China's first hydrogen bomb test in 1967 is mentioned as history; its design is not public and is not drawn.

The fitted residual is not the accuracy of the fire boundary; no destruction-area statistic is derived.
- The persistent “about 2 km, nearly 3 km north” is an approximate reading of the main mapped fire area. It is not a circular perimeter, not a claim that every direction extends that far, and excludes small detached patches. Fire extent, structural damage, thermal exposure and radiation dose are distinct.

## Fixed macroscopic effects

`city-effects.ts` replaces the former illustrative scale relations. It only supplies four fixed yields around historical Hiroshima. There is no arbitrary location, address search, target model, casualty calculator or burst-height optimiser.

**Historical version:** [RERF RR 7-06 / DS02](https://www.rerf.or.jp/en/library/archives-en/scientific_pub/rrtoc/rr2006-en/rr0607-en/) revises Hiroshima to 16 kt / 600 m. These are historical estimates. Other presets scale that historical height by the cube root of the yield ratio, solely to compare similar fixed scenarios. These are neither each weapon's actual deployment conditions nor optimal heights.

### Round 3: thermal check under the book's stated conditions

The map now uses the **1977 Fig.7.42 reference itself**, replacing the previously displayed Soviet IVC9 curve. We did not relabel IVC9 as 12-mile visibility or fit a multiplier to make it agree. The chart assumes a cloudless atmosphere with **12-mile visibility** and supplies approximately applicable results for airburst heights up to **15,000 ft** (§§7.42,7.99). It includes atmospheric transmittance and scattered radiation via Eq.7.96, Table7.88 and Fig.7.98; it is not the vacuum inverse-square law.

Manual 8 cal/cm² curve knots (yield kt, slant miles): **(1,0.35), (10,1.1), (100,3.0), (500,5.5), (2000,9.1), (10000,15.0)**. Values are deliberately rounded to the scan's graphical precision. Interpolation is linear in log yield/log range. The model only supplies this fixed exposure reference; it is not a general atmospheric solver. A 5% graphical tolerance is declared in advance. The following 20, 50 and 1000 kt checks are **not interpolation knots**. The 1000 kt entry uses the printed worked example, rather than reading the same curve back at a knot.

| Same conditions: 8 cal/cm², 12-mile visibility, cloudless, H ≤15,000 ft | Original reference slant range (mi) | Interpolated model (mi) | Difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 20 kt, manual Fig.7.42 reading | ≈1.5 | 1.48785 | −0.81% |
| 50 kt, manual Fig.7.42 reading | ≈2.2 | 2.21796 | +0.82% |
| 1000 kt, printed §7.43 worked example | ≈7.0 | 7.0746 | +1.07% |

These comparisons check transcription/interpolation under the **same stated book conditions**; they do not independently validate the book's atmospheric physics. Manual readings are approximate; the two graph readings are not independent experiments. Differences of this size are below the declared 5% reading tolerance and are consistent with rounded graph readings and sparse interpolation. Tests also check the slant-to-ground projection and refuse extrapolation outside 1–10,000 kt or the stated height limit. Historical Hiroshima still uses DS02 16 kt / 600 m; comparison heights retain the previous fixed similarity scaling.

For transparency, at the book example's 1 Mt / 7 mi slant input, the **old IVC9 function returns 20.37 cal/cm²**, compared with the book's approximately 8 cal/cm² (**+154.6%**). This is **not a same-atmosphere test**: the old clear-air nomogram and the book's 12-mile visibility differ, as do their empirical approximations. The available sources do not support attributing the entire discrepancy quantitatively to visibility. Its regression agreement with upstream did not validate its use as a 1977 book model. The old function remains only for traceable regression tests, and does not determine the visible contour.

The displayed radius is a ground distance, derived from chart slant range using `sqrt(slant² − height²)`, not the chart slant distance itself. Approximate ground radii are 2.09 km (16 kt), 4.49 km (90 kt), and 11.90 km (1200 kt); the 50 Mt preset exceeds both the chart yield and height domains and has no thermal ring. The 8 cal/cm² curve is an exposure reference, **not an ignition, skin injury, historical fire or casualty boundary**. The interface retains an explicit model label and states the book conditions.

Pressure contours sample outward along the fixed scenario and interpolate a threshold crossing; this is a rendering step, not a search for destructive conditions. Rings assume a flat surface and a standard atmosphere. Buildings, shelter, terrain, fire spread, weather and dose are not simulated. Radius and area multipliers are separately labelled; neither is a casualty multiplier.

Preset public facts: W76-1 approximately 90 kt from [FAS US Nuclear Weapons 2025](https://fas.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/United-States-nuclear-weapons-2025.pdf); B83-1 up to 1.2 Mt from [UCS fact sheet](https://ucs-documents.s3.amazonaws.com/nuclear-weapons/b83-fact-sheet.pdf); 50 Mt Tsar Bomba historical reference from the existing [1961 history entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsar_Bomba). Only these yield facts are used, not internal or delivery specifications.

**Residual radiation:** [RERF FAQ](https://www.rerf.or.jp/en/faq/) distinguishes radioactive fallout from neutron activation. Black rain is a historical precipitation phenomenon involving soot and other matter; fallout is deposition of radioactive matter, sometimes with precipitation; activation makes irradiated surface materials radioactive. Airbursts can leave residual radioactivity. This version draws no fallout, black-rain or activation boundary and includes no WSEG-10 calculation.

## An era: data and precision

- **OWID / Arms Control Association, CC BY 4.0:** [annual tests](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/number-of-nuclear-weapons-tests), [full origin metadata, indicator 1229477](https://api.ourworldindata.org/v1/indicators/1229477.metadata.json), based on Daryl Kimball, *The Nuclear Testing Tally*. Origin metadata explicitly declares CC BY 4.0 and `nonRedistributable: false`, rechecked 2026-10-26. Dataset version 2026-05-13, observations 1945–2024. The chart excludes Hiroshima/Nagasaki and Vela and counts some simultaneous detonations as one test. Missing years after 2024 are not zeros.
- **OWID / Federation of American Scientists, CC BY 4.0:** [stockpiles](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/nuclear-warhead-stockpiles), [origin metadata 1229473](https://api.ourworldindata.org/v1/indicators/1229473.metadata.json). Existing `history-data.json` snapshot (1945–2026, version 2026-05-13) retained without changing observations. Country stockpiles exclude retired weapons awaiting dismantlement; the separate OWID World series includes them and is not summed into this chart. The country-sum peak is computed from the rows: 1986, 64,452. Tests peak: 1962, 178; USA 96, USSR 79, UK 2, France 1. Counts and stockpiles have separate linear vertical axes and one shared year cursor. These are inventories, not measures of destructive power.
- CSV SHA-256, preserved in the snapshot: stockpiles `5fa030e5a7e312c74884adf5d9154afef16fd2fc7fe6b33eaa6ee72401dd7a9b`; tests `4a184bf157d180c91f71e1817f922bf571861b5961b964e0ca758a9101907984`.
- **US Department of Energy, US government work / public domain:** [DOE/NV-209 Rev.16, September 2015](https://nnss.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/DOE_NV-209_Rev16.pdf), *United States Nuclear Tests July 1945 through September 1992*. `data/extract-doe.py` extracts the Tests By Name table into downloadable `doe-events.json`: name, UTC calendar date, published coordinates, test identifier and PDF page. Engineering columns are discarded. There are 1,054 distinct tests and 1,149 detonations; one coordinate is unavailable and omitted from the globe. No coordinate jitter or national-total allocation is used. The annual ACA series and DOE detonations deliberately retain their different definitions.
- **Other countries:** `era-sites.ts` is a small directory of public geographic facts and use intervals, with per-entry source links to DOE and the [UN historical overview](https://www.un.org/en/observances/end-nuclear-tests-day/history). Rounded centres (0.1°) are labelled **site level**, not individual detonation positions. This directory is incomplete; it is not a licensed global event database, and no per-site annual counts have been manufactured. The generic overview supports site history, not precision coordinates; centres are approximate geographic labels. Intervals do not assert activity in every year. Unknown site counts remain unknown; national totals are not allocated to sites. Small markers are site locations, including missing counts. Known US site marker disc areas represent annual distinct DOE test identifiers; the fine cumulative coordinate layer remains individual detonation positions, so its density is not a test count.
- **Round 3 site activity:** `era-activity.ts` groups only the published DOE labels `NNSS` / `NNSS U9ITS`, `Bikini Atoll`, `Enewetak Atoll`, test #1 (Trinity), and `Island area` records in documented Kiritimati and Johnston operating regions. The bounds identify the two separate island operations (Kiritimati: 155–160°W, 0–3°N; Johnston region: 165–175°W, 10–20°N); they do not fabricate event coordinates or spread national totals. Johnston and Kiritimati are operating-region markers: offshore shots remain at their published coordinates. Uncovered DOE sites/offshore records remain real fine points without being assigned to the nearest marker. Marker counts deduplicate `testNumber`, including simultaneous detonations. Kiritimati counts cover **US DOE records only**; its UK use is explicitly labelled as outside these counts. Other foreign per-site annual/cumulative counts remain unknown.
- **1962 audit:** DOE has 98 distinct test identifiers: Nevada 62, Kiritimati region 24, Johnston region 10, plus Frigate Bird and Swordfish at their published offshore positions (no invented site centre). Country totals in the existing OWID chart remain USA 96, UK 2, USSR 79, France 1. DOE Table 1 separates US and US–UK totals; its 1962 entries Pampas (#215) and Tendrac (#299) are marked Joint US–UK; the annual sources assign the two joint tests to the UK. These two definitions are displayed separately, not forced to match. Clicking a site pauses playback and shows its name, country, current-year count, and cumulative count through the selected year.
- **Camera and motion:** the busiest country is chosen from the OWID annual series. For US-led years, focus is the spherical centroid of that year's published DOE coordinates, one per distinct test; this points at the Pacific/US West in 1962, without inventing a busiest location. For other countries the camera uses an unweighted overview of known sites in use, because per-site annual counts are unavailable. It does not claim which foreign site led that year. No-activity/missing years retain the last view. Motion eases toward the focus; dragging gives manual control and replay restores following. An active known marker gently pulses once at each year change; reduced-motion settings disable the pulse and easing. Unknown counts do not flash as confirmed activity.
- **Natural Earth, public domain:** [official China POV](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/blog/admin-0-countries-point-of-views/), [5.1.1 source ZIP](https://naturalearth.s3.amazonaws.com/10m_cultural/ne_10m_admin_0_countries_chn.zip), [terms](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/). `earth-china.geojson` is a 0.08° simplified, four-decimal derived overview. Geometry, not just colour, uses the official China POV. Tests verify Taipei, Tawang and Itanagar fall inside the China feature. Only geometry is used, not the source's de-facto-based area/population fields. Canvas colours, light and porcelain material are original.

The prior historical milestone data remains in `history.ts`; its individual reference URLs are shown for milestones matching the selected year. 596 and the 1967 Chinese thermonuclear milestone appear only as historical facts. Round 3 does not change the existing device schematics or chain-reaction illustrations.
