Copied runtime modules: `sim`, `renderer`, `gpu`, `noise`, `camera`, `scene`, `blackbody`, `sparks`, `shaders/*`, `audio/{engine,worklet,fire-dsp}`. No upstream server, capture, Puppeteer or offline mastering tools enter the website.

Local engine patches:

- `renderer.js`: destroy per-resize uniform buffers; optional pass timestamps; cache static-camera surface lighting for three of every four desktop frames, invalidating on camera/size/cut changes; clip volume integration to the retained half-space. Existing cubic reconstruction and TAA remain.
- `shaders/scene.js`: intersect wood SDFs and their shadows with the retained half-space; shade newly exposed caps with procedural growth rings. This is geometry, not a solved internal wood temperature.
- `sim.js`: add a passive diagnostic texture `reaction` storing burned fuel fraction / dt and pre-reaction gas temperature. No source, reaction, transport, cooling, or pressure equation changed.
- `sparks.js`: expose a multiplier on the existing gravitational acceleration for the gravity control; the default multiplier is 1.
- `gpu.js`: suppress the expected `device.lost` error on explicit destruction (React unmount / StrictMode).
- `audio/engine.js`: bundle the worklet and its DSP import with Vite `?worker&url` in production; use its module URL during development because Vite's development Worker wrapper uses `importScripts`, which is unavailable inside an AudioWorklet.

`runtime.js` is a separate host adapter: lifecycle/disposal, touch orbit and pinch, actual simulation timestep scaling, startup preroll, adaptive pixel resolution, sound gesture, and the GPU section/probe. It uses upstream real-time medium/low grids and `tools/render_final.mjs`'s `FINAL` laminar/render parameters, with source-only calibration in `calibration.ts`. These are visual calibration parameters, not new chemistry. The upstream combustion equations are unchanged.

`section.js` samples original fields on the GPU. A probe reads five trilinear local stencils into an 80-byte buffer (one in flight, normally ≤9 reads/s). It never copies the whole volume to the CPU. Section colors are independent of the camera's flame tone mapper. Switching to natural color preserves the plane and probe; the natural image integrates a ray and is not a unique voxel measurement.

## Channel contract (verified against `sim.js`, not only upstream DESIGN.md)

Domain: 1.2 × 1.8 × 1.2 m; origin (−0.6,0,−0.6), y up. Medium grid 80×120×80, h=0.015 m; low 64×96×64, h=0.01875 m. All five textures are `rgba16float`.

| Texture / channel | Stored meaning | Unit / displayed conversion | Qualification |
|---|---|---|---|
| S.r | θ, gas temperature above ambient divided by 1000 | T = 293 + 1000θ K; °C = K − 273.15 | Post-cooling temperature; no solid-wood temperature |
| S.g | f, lumped pyrolysis fuel | dimensionless, nominal mass fraction; display ×100 % | No individual CH₄/H₂/CO concentrations |
| S.b | soot volume fraction | ppm; ×10⁻⁶ gives volume fraction | Reduced soot model, not particles or a species mechanism |
| S.a | q / 10⁴, combustion temperature increment / dt | ×10⁴ K/s | Pre-radiative-cooling increment, capped by 2100 K. Not W/m³ or whole-fire kW |
| S2.r | P, products **plus source inert gas** | dimensionless carrier fraction | Includes imposed source dilution; decays numerically. Not a CO₂ fraction |
| S2.g | fuel-derived gas age weighted by Z=f+P | stored Z·s; age = channel/Z for Z>10⁻⁴ | Age clamped to 4 s; fresh ambient has no defined age |
| S2.b | M, organic smoke tracer | model units | DESIGN suggests 100 mg/m³ units, but source/decay in code are not mass-calibrated; do not display that as measured concentration |
| S2.a | coal-bed CO-like fuel tag | dimensionless 0–1 | Inhibits soot; not an independently conserved CO concentration. DESIGN's tar interpretation is stale |
| vel.rgb | projected velocity vector | m/s | Magnitude displayed; vectors projected onto section for arrows |
| vel.a | 0 after projection | none in final vel | Expansion φ is in intermediate velX.a, s⁻¹; final vel.a is **not** divergence |
| vort.rgb | half central velocity differences in cell index space | divide xyz by h to obtain curl, s⁻¹ | From advected velocity **before** sources/forces/projection |
| vort.a | length of stored curl vector | divide by h, s⁻¹ | For a point, we interpolate xyz then take length; not interpolate precomputed magnitudes |
| geo.r | hot log pyrolysis-band weight | dimensionless | Cached source geometry |
| geo.g | coal-bed weight | dimensionless | Not coal temperature or mass |
| geo.b | solid-log mask | 0 / 1 (interpolated at probe) | >0.5 is hatched and gas readouts withheld |
| geo.a | closest modified log signed distance | m | Negative inside; rendering SDF detail differs from simulation capsules |

Derived quantities:

- Available air `o = clamp(1 − f − P,0,1)`. This is the **air carrier**, not O₂ fraction. An explicitly labeled approximation is `Y_O2 ≈ 0.232 o`, assuming fresh air's oxygen mass fraction. Do not confuse mass fraction with the roughly 21% mole fraction.
- Upstream `main.js profile()` estimates a thermal power density `(354800/T) q` W/m³. This is an ideal-gas ρcp proxy using post-cooling T, not a chemical HRR measurement. It is now integrated over gas cells and explicitly labeled a thermal proxy.
- Source and cooling scalars are calibrated graphical approximations. Fuel, P and soot have imposed dissipative terms. There is no full conserved-species chemistry, solid conduction/pyrolysis evolution, depletion, water, detailed radiation transport, or molecular diffusion.
- Actual soot age gate is **15–50 ms**, temperature gate **1000–1400 K**. The 20–60 ms / 1300–1800 K ranges in DESIGN are an earlier proposal. `heatOut=7700 K`, `rs=4.7`, `ignT=550 K`, `kMix=30 s⁻¹`. Defaults use a constant-density projection with an expansion source and McCaffrey-inspired buoyancy. Calling it a validated variable-density fire prediction code would overstate it.
- 60 kW, 0.6 m flame height and 1.94 Hz are upstream design targets. They have not been independently validated as simultaneous measured properties of this interactive configuration. No UI counter fabricates them.

## Scientific and design references

- [McCaffrey 1979, NBSIR 79-1910](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/IR/nbsir79-1910.pdf): buoyant diffusion-flame profiles; upstream buoyancy calibration, not proof of our solver's validation.
- [Xia & Zhang, 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10400): vortex dynamics and flicker scaling; basis for the separately labeled flicker prediction.
- [Nguyen, Fedkiw & Jensen 2002](https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/fire-sg02/): physically based fire animation lineage.
- [NIST Smokeview](https://pages.nist.gov/fds/): established field slices and vectors; a research comparator, no code/assets copied.
- [Smith & van der Walt, Inferno](https://github.com/BIDS/colormap/blob/master/colormaps.py): CC0/public-domain color values, five-stop interpolation for temperature and heating. Cool sequential map is project-authored. Five-stop interpolation is an approximation of Inferno, not its full 256-entry map.

Visuals, sparks and audio are generated by the engine; no external texture or audio samples. Poster derivatives come from the upstream MIT preview. A silent loop, when supplied through `/assets/campfire/campfire-loop.mp4`, is rendered from this same engine and supplied separately as a binary asset. Browser feature detection requires both WebGPU and the formats/filtering features the upstream renderer uses; browser brand alone is insufficient.


## Measurement and display contract (updated in round 4)

- Default grid is upstream `low` (64×96×64, dx=0.01875 m, dt=1/60 s); `med` (80×120×80, dt=1/75 s) remains a quality option. Desktop internal DPR is capped at 1, mobile at 1.25, surface and volume at half internal dimensions. Physics advances with fixed upstream steps; slow motion multiplies each step by 0.1–1, never enlarging it to catch the wall clock. At most two steps and two queued frames prevent unbounded work. Background tabs suspend progression.
- A vertical cut retains the side away from the camera; a horizontal cut retains the lower half. The simulation still runs across the whole domain. This is a viewing cut, not removal of fuel/oxygen. The cut field defaults to opacity 1. The complete plane remains visible: cold air uses a dark 30%-opacity glass grid, transitioning between 450–900 K to opaque field color. A fine border defines its extent. The opacity control scales both. Available-air and flow fields retain 35% of their field color in cold air, and velocity arrows remain bright, so entrainment remains legible. Undefined gas age retains the glass without inventing a color value. Solid wood is excluded from gas colors so the geometric cut caps remain visible. Soot/temperature colors use raw fields, not the rendered flame's tone mapping or spatial warp.
- Gas age is `S2.g/(S.g+S2.r)` in seconds, displayed in ms; no defined age is shown for near-zero carrier. The actual soot gate is 15–50 ms, also requiring the 1000–1400 K temperature gate. The optional, labeled ×40 blue display changes only `blueK` from 0.05 to 2; it is off by default and never affects probe values.
- `reaction.r = b/dt` is consumed nominal fuel fraction per second; `reaction.g = T0` K is pre-reaction temperature. They use `rgba16float` like the original fields; `reaction.ba=0`. The solid mask excludes invalid gas cells from reductions.
- Chemical power **estimate**: `Σ [(353/T0) (b/dt) (14e6 J/kg) ΔV] / 1000` kW. Ideal-air density at about one atmosphere and an assumed effective heat of 14 MJ/kg are explicit assumptions, not additional species solved by the model. The 14 MJ/kg wood value is adopted from upstream `research/physics.md` §1; applying it to the lumped pyrolysis-gas scalar is an approximation. This is not independently validated calorimetry.
- Thermal power **proxy**: `Σ [(354800/T_after) q ΔV] / 1000` kW, using the upstream profile convention. Solver `heatOut=7700 K` corresponds to roughly 7.7 MJ/kg for cp≈1000 J/(kg·K), before the 2100 K reaction cap. It therefore need not agree with the 14 MJ/kg estimate; post-cooling versus pre-reaction density also differs. Neither curve is filled from the 60 kW target.
- `meters.js` performs a GPU reduction per horizontal layer, then a whole-domain GPU reduction. Only 192 bytes are read at 4 Hz (powers, heights, feature locations, 120 packed layer flags, thermal-boundary diagnostics and spark state). Five frequency signals use a second GPU reduction plus three centreline temperatures, reading 208 bytes at nominal 20 Hz. No full-volume readback occurs. Statistics use simulation seconds, including slow motion and pause.
- Visible-flame mask: T>1000 K and soot>0.02 ppm, OR T>900 K and q>3000 K/s and age<50 ms. For **visible** height, apply the original renderer's side (0.02–0.15 m) and top (0.4 m) boundary fade to temperature excess, soot and q first. Require at least 0.0005 m² above threshold in a layer. This is a declared model visibility criterion, not an experimental camera luminance threshold; it excludes detail warp and tone mapping. The raw, unfaded highest layer and top-boundary temperatures are retained for diagnostics. Neither the height nor power is derived from an assumed 60 kW target.
- Height is the highest layer occupied by the flame mask during ≥50% of samples in the latest **40 simulation seconds** (4 Hz), measured from y=0.05 m. This replaces round 2’s median highest-tip proxy, which counted empty space below detached puffs. A full window is required. [NIST SP 1034](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=861329) describes 50% intermittency. It remains a declared computational flame mask, not calibrated photographic visibility.
- Heskestad prediction is `L=0.235 Q^0.4−1.02D`, Q in kW, lengths in m, using the contemporaneous mean chemical estimate and an **assumed equivalent diameter D=0.60 m**. Multiple logs are not a circular pool fire. See [NIST TN 1984](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/TechnicalNotes/NIST.TN.1984.pdf). The formula is shown alongside measured height, never used to generate it.
- Frequency uses chemical power, instantaneous threshold height and centreline gas T at y=0.25/0.50/0.80 m. About 20 Hz is resampled to 800 points over 40 s, linearly detrended and Hann-windowed. DFT searches 0.2–4 Hz at 0.025 Hz spacing. A peak must be interior, ≥8× median background and ≥18% of band power, with another signal agreeing within 0.075 Hz. Missing, flat or broadband signals yield no stable peak. The separate `1.5/√D` prediction is never fed into this estimator.
- The two comparison buttons locate actual current grid features (young, low-soot reacting gas near the base; hot soot-rich gas above it). They move the plane and camera, then use the same local probe. They are not fixed illustrative readings. Yellow need not be cooler than the selected root in every frame; the conclusion concerns soot, age and line-of-sight brightness, not a universal temperature ranking.
- Anatomy readings are gas fields or current spark state. Wood reads nearby fuel gas, char reads gas above the bed, smoke reads its uncalibrated tracer. No solid temperature, CO₂ fraction, or fabricated particle is presented as measured.

The UI is the dark “night” direction only. Eight compact annotation stations share short bilingual copy with the tour. Tip anchors reuse the 20 Hz frequency reduction (other feature anchors update at 4 Hz) using the highest layer whose local soot emission is ≥0.2% of the current peak and ≥0.0002 relative to the upstream 1500 K blackbody reference. They use the engine’s blackbody LUT, not only temperature: a hot cell can be visually dark. This locates the gas sample and camera target, not the height statistics. The leader endpoint is aligned to the actual reconstructed volume contour by `visible-tip.js`: first pair of adjacent image rows with exposed linear red >0.15 (raised in round 4 to exclude barely visible wisps). This GPU image reduction reads only 16 bytes at up to 20 Hz while annotations are visible. It excludes wood/sparks, reads no whole image, invalidates on camera/size changes, and does not feed back into any physics or height statistic. The card explicitly says “soot near the tip”: the line-of-sight contour and a nearby original gas cell are different measurements. Smoke anchors are non-flaming tracer cells above the threshold flame top; logs and char use fixed geometry anchors.

Lifecycle: expected cancellation of pending GPU readbacks during quality changes/unmount is ignored after disposal. Saved comparison points pause the solver first and sample the frozen field, so the saved and current readings share the same simulation instant.


## Round 3: source calibration and experiments

- Round 3 source parameters (superseded by round 4 below): `intensity=0.30`, `fuelSrc=0.15`, `bedFuel=0.015`, `srcTemp=0.75`, `bedTemp=0.35`. They change existing hot-gas supply only. The previous `FINAL` laminar/source-patch geometry, all combustion/cooling/transport equations and default Earth buoyancy remain. The calibration is to a smaller illustrative fire, not independent validation of the solver.
- “Add fuel” scales source intensity by 1–2×; it does not add log geometry. “Remove fuel” sets both wood and bed gas sources, smolder and burst fuel to zero. Intensity alone is insufficient because the upstream bed has a minimum intensity multiplier. Existing gas and particles continue evolving; no new sparks spawn. Fixed hot-source/material temperatures remain, so red embers are not a solved solid-cooling experiment.
- An eight-simulation-second gust scales the existing windX/windZ/gust forces with a half sine. It changes transport and mixing, without prescribing heat or oxygen results. Air on the slice remains `clamp(1−f−P,0,1)`, not an independently solved oxygen species.
- Gravity scales the existing buoyancy coefficient `26.8*g` and the spark gravitational force `9.81*g`; upstream source upflow and turbulence remain. There is **no molecular diffusion**, so this cannot reproduce a NASA spherical microgravity flame. [NASA’s 1g/microgravity candle comparison](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/candle-flame-1g-vs-microgravity/) is linked beside the control. No NASA imagery is bundled.
- Human-readable power equivalents use the same chemical estimate: `Q_kW/14` g/s of equivalent gas and `Q_kW/2` two-kilowatt kettles. They are conversions under stated assumptions, not independent fuel-weighing measurements.

## Molecular explanation layer

- Five original ball-and-stick scenes use the existing [Three.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js) dependency (MIT) and original coordinates/topology; no downloaded molecular model, photograph or illustration. Carbon dark grey, hydrogen off-white, oxygen red. Atom identities persist across interpolation and bond changes. These are explanatory models, not an atomistic trajectory or species readback.
- The probe chooses a station from actual fuel, available air, temperature, soot, age, reaction rate and geometry. Users may change stations. The physical fire pauses while the modal is open and resumes its prior state on return.
- Methane: CH₄ + 2 O₂ → CO₂ + 2 H₂O, nine atoms throughout (C 1, H 4, O 4); the actual lumped pyrolysis gas is a mixture. Intermediate radical reactions are omitted. Carbon oxidation shows the balanced C + O₂ → CO₂ path, with the CO route explained but not implied to have been measured.
- Wood shows one C₆H₁₀O₅ cellulose repeat with polymer ends omitted and illustrative bond cleavage. Fragments are not claimed as unique pyrolysis products. Soot shows carbon frameworks approaching, not a complete soot mechanism. Exhaust shows possible species without measured proportions.
- Bond breaking requires energy; bond formation releases it. Net combustion releases stored chemical energy originally supplied by photosynthesis.
- [USDA Wood Handbook, Fire Safety chapter, p.17–7 and table 17–2](https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/7146.pdf) reports gross combustion heat typically **about 20 MJ/kg**; the table’s wood samples span 19.8–21.1 MJ/kg and effective values depend on exposure. The short copy’s “roughly 18–20 MJ/kg for oven-dry wood” is an approximate educational range around that scale, not a verbatim USDA bound. It is explicitly distinguished from this model’s **14 MJ/kg effective assumption**, which is also applied approximately to its lumped gas. No uncertain species concentration or universal pyrolysis threshold is published.


## Round 4: camera, source layout and candle illustration

- Three source-only alternatives remain in `calibration.ts`; development previews use `?candidate=warm|wide|low`. The selected default lowers the upper ends of the five leaning logs to 65% of their previous height above the bed. Identical packed capsules go to the source geometry and renderer. Fuel supply covers more log surface; it does not add heat or light independently of the existing source equations. All pressure, reaction, soot, cooling and transport equations are unchanged. Domain and grid spacing remain unchanged.
- Camera-style automatic exposure meters the mean RGB intensity of the existing eight virtual point lights, a relative linear-light quantity (not watts). Every 0.25 simulation seconds it reads 256 bytes already provided by the renderer. Target exposure is `clamp(candidateExposure * sqrt(0.126 / max(lightMean, 1e-5)), 28, 42)`, approaching it with a 2.5-second time constant. This photographic adaptation affects the image only; it cannot alter temperature, soot, power or probe values. It freezes during pause. The upstream blackbody/CIE color calculation, tone mapping, bloom, TAA and procedural detail remain.
- Offscreen anatomy measurements keep their actual 3D position and readings. Only their projected leader endpoint is clamped to the safe screen rectangle; an arrow indicates the out-of-view direction. Missing current smoke or spark measurements leave a dim, reachable label with no fabricated reading.
- The candle scene is an **optical illustration, not a fluid/chemistry simulation**. It migrates this project's previous AGPL-3.0 `fire-gravity/flame-shader.ts`, with twelve depth samples and correspondingly normalized emission. Procedural wax, wick, light and layers use no third-party textures. Ordinary-gravity flame shape, layer colors, flow marks and the microgravity dome are explanatory. No temperature, power, fuel fraction or probe reading is manufactured from the shader.
- A same-equation wick-source experiment at 8×12×8 cm failed the scale assessment: halving the step strongly changed inferred power, and the flame reached the domain top. A plausible-looking 75 W sample was therefore insufficient evidence of a candle solver. Source injection, numerical mixing and the absent molecular diffusion prevent claiming convergence at that scale. The illustration is explicitly disclosed in both languages.
- [Hamins, Bundy & Dillon, *Characterization of Candle Flames*, 2005](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=101159): the reference candle is a 21-mm paraffin candle with **77±9 W** heat release and **42±1 mm** flame height (p.277). These are literature measurements, not measurements of this scene. Its fuel heat is around 43 MJ/kg; the campfire's 14 MJ/kg assumption is not applied to this reference candle.
- Candle layers: wax melts, rises along the wick and vaporizes; fuel-rich vapor near the core burns mainly after mixing with surrounding air. Soot emits yellow light in the luminous region; the surrounding oxidation region is usually hotter. These are qualitative regions, not three universal temperature constants. Methane is explicitly a simpler hydrocarbon example, not the composition of wax. The balanced example preserves C 1, H 4 and O 4 on both sides; H/O/OH radical chemistry is explanatory text only.
- [NASA 1g/microgravity candle comparison](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/candle-flame-1g-vs-microgravity/) and [NASA USML-1 experiment record](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20030075810): in quiescent microgravity, diffusion dominates and a candle can settle into a dim blue hemispherical flame after its initial transient. The illustrated dome is labeled “illustration, based on NASA observations”; no NASA image is bundled or represented as solver output.
- The retired `/fire-gravity` and `/en/fire-gravity` paths use the existing server redirect mechanism, with a scene alias to `/campfire?scene=candle` and its English equivalent. Query parameters are retained. Campfire remains `making`.
- The replacement silent loop is generated locally from this same default source, camera and automatic exposure; its offline renderer spends extra samples on surface/volume quality. It contains no additional fire textures or changed simulation equations. Binary delivery and R2 upload remain separate from source integration.
